The tech industry is eating itself alive.

52% of the internet is now AI-generated content. (Graphite, 2025)
"Slop" — AI-generated junk — was Merriam-Webster's Word of the Year 2025.
78% of AI startups are literally just API wrappers. (CB Insights, 2024)

We built the most powerful creative tools in history.
Then immediately used them to flood the world with garbage.

Here's the full breakdown — and why this ends badly for everyone building right now 🧵

Let's talk about the piracy problem nobody wants to say out loud.

Meta downloaded 81 terabytes of books from Anna's Archive — a pirated library — to train their models. NVIDIA contacted Anna's Archive directly to access 500TB of pirated data for NeMo and InstructRetro. This came out in unsealed court documents.

Getty Images is suing Stability AI for $1.7 billion.
The RIAA sued Suno and Udio for $150,000 per infringed work.
The New York Times is suing OpenAI.

In 2023, Ghostwriter977 dropped a Drake deepfake — "Heart on My Sleeve" — using AI-cloned vocals. 9M TikTok shares. 250K Spotify streams. Pulled by Universal Music Group two weeks later.

The boring truth: the AI content boom was built on unlicensed work. The legal bills are arriving.

We didn't invent new creativity. We laundered someone else's.

Now add the pseudo-creativity layer.

95% of generative AI pilots in enterprise delivered zero measurable financial return. (MIT NANDA report, July 2025 — 300 deployments analyzed)

42% of enterprise AI projects were abandoned in 2025 — up from 17% the year before. (S&P Global, 1,006 companies surveyed)

Why?

Because most of them are adding unnecessary flavor instead of solving real problems.

AI-generated thumbnails that look slightly off. AI-written emails that sound like AI. AI customer service bots that gaslight users and escalate tickets. AI "summaries" that miss the entire point of the document.

We took genuine capability — LLMs that can reason, generate, retrieve — and used it to produce content that satisfies a checklist instead of a human.

Capability without judgment isn't innovation. It's noise at scale.

Here's where the corporate law angle gets ugly.

California SB 1047 would have required AI safety testing before deployment. Big Tech spent millions lobbying. Newsom vetoed it in September 2024.

Patent troll activity grew 15–20% in 2025 vs. 2024. (Unified Patents) Shell companies filing broad AI patents — not to build anything, just to extract licensing fees from companies that actually are building.

WordPress rejected 86% of DMCA notices in H2 2025 — defective, automated, or AI-generated. Small creators without legal teams can't fight back the same way.

The pattern: companies with one overwhelming strength — lawyers, lobbyists, or capital — are using that single card to lock in the whole game.

They didn't win by building better. They won by making it expensive for everyone else to build at all.

That's not competition. That's capture.

So what do you actually do with this if you're building?

1. Don't build on stolen foundations. Use licensed training data or open-weight models with clean provenance (Llama, Mistral). The lawsuits will eventually catch up — and they'll find the companies underneath too.

2. Reject pseudo-creativity. If your AI feature saves someone 0 minutes per week, kill it. If users immediately re-edit the output, kill it. Ship things that work for real humans.

3. Watch the regulatory capture playbook. The companies blocking safety legislation aren't doing it because regulation is bad. They're doing it because compliance costs are a moat — and they can afford it, you can't.

4. Build moats that can't be lobbied away. Proprietary data. Real user relationships. Evaluations the big players can't replicate.

The tech that survives this phase isn't the most viral. It's the most defensible.
