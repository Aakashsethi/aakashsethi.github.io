POST 1/5:

Side projects don't get you hired.

Tutorial projects with a fresh coat of paint don't either.

I reviewed 200+ junior resumes last year. 90% had the same 3 projects: a to-do app, a weather API, a Netflix clone.

Zero callbacks. Zero interviews. Zero offers.

Here's what I learned 🧵

---

POST 2/5:

The boring truth: recruiters don't care about your tech stack.

They care about one thing — did you solve a problem someone actually has?

A to-do app solves nothing. There are 10,000 to-do apps.

A Netflix clone proves you can follow a YouTube tutorial. That's not engineering. That's transcription.

I built NoshSource in 2020. It hit 5,000 daily users.

Not because the code was elegant. Because hungry people in my city needed cheap food during COVID, and I gave it to them.

That one project got me more interviews than my entire GitHub combined.

---

POST 3/5:

The turning point for me was realizing this:

Engineers who get hired build things that have users.

Not stars on GitHub. Users. Real humans who open the app twice a week.

Once I understood that, my project filter changed completely.

Before: "What's a cool tech stack to learn?"
After: "What's a problem 100 people would pay $5/month to solve?"

The second question is harder. That's the point.

Most juniors skip it because it's uncomfortable. They'd rather rebuild Twitter for the 4,000th time.

---

POST 4/5:

If I were starting today, I'd build projects using this framework:

1. Pick a niche you already live in (gym, college, gaming, faith community, immigrant community).

2. Find 10 people in that niche. Ask what wastes their time weekly.

3. Build the ugliest possible v1 in 2 weeks. Ship it.

4. Get 50 real users. Not friends. Strangers.

5. Add ONE AI feature that actually helps (RAG over their data, automated workflows, smart search). Not a ChatGPT wrapper.

6. Write a case study: problem → users → metrics → tech decisions.

7. Put the case study at the top of your resume. Not the bottom.

This beats 4 tutorial projects every single time.

---

POST 5/5:

Recap:

→ Tutorial projects signal that you can follow instructions.
→ User-facing projects signal that you can think.
→ Recruiters skim for signals. Give them the right one.

Build 1 project with 50 real users instead of 5 projects with 0.

I spent 4 years learning this the hard way so you don't have to.
